Tagesschau Broadcast Corpus and Migration Salience

Ongoing · Independent research · Since 2025 · Sole author

Public concern about immigration in Germany has surged and receded in ways that asylum numbers alone cannot explain. Concern peaked with record asylum applications in 1992/93 and 2015/16 — but barely moved when a million Ukrainians arrived in 2022. Does television news mirror real-world change, drive concern beyond it, or follow its audience? Reality, coverage, and public opinion have to be modelled as a system.

The empirical basis is a full-text corpus of the spoken Tagesschau um 20 Uhr (ARD), Germany’s most-watched television news programme — arguably the closest approximation to a shared national information environment. No comparable public corpus of the broadcast exists.

The corpus

12,522 broadcast days · ~3,100 hours · 01/1991 – 07/2026 · 15-month archive gap 2006/07 · transcribed locally with Whisper

† The corpus itself cannot be published (ARD broadcast rights); the processing pipeline can.

Validation

The salience measure is validated against the hand-coded airtime shares of the IFEM InfoMonitor. These reach back only to 2005; earlier decades are checked with era-specific vocabularies.

Three migration episodes

The puzzle: public concern tracked the first two episodes but barely moved for the third.

  • 1992/93

    Asylum debate. Record asylum applications; concern peaked.

  • 2015/16

    Refugee inflow. Record inflow; concern peaked again.

  • 2022

    Ukraine displacement. A million arrivals; concern barely moved.

Method

  1. Scraping — retrieval of 12,522 broadcasts, 01/1991 – 07/2026
  2. Whisper ASR — local transcription of ~3,100 hours of audio
  3. Transcription diagnostics — automated quality checks, against official broadcast subtitles where available
  4. Salience measure — monthly, dictionary-based; era-specific vocabularies pre-2005; validated against IFEM InfoMonitor
  5. Trivariate VAR with Granger-causality tests — ~400 monthly observations, alongside BAMF asylum statistics and the Politbarometer “most important problems” series. Granger tests establish temporal precedence, not full causal identification
