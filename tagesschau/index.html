Tagesschau Broadcast Corpus and Migration Salience

Independent researchSince 2025Sole author

Public concern about immigration in Germany has surged and receded in ways that asylum numbers alone cannot explain. Concern peaked with record asylum applications in 1992/93 and 2015/16 — but barely moved when a million Ukrainians arrived in 2022. Does television news mirror real-world change, drive concern beyond it, or follow its audience? Reality, coverage, and public opinion have to be modelled as a system.

The empirical basis is a full-text corpus of the spoken Tagesschau um 20 Uhr (ARD), Germany's most-watched television news programme — arguably the closest approximation to a shared national information environment. No comparable public corpus of the broadcast exists.

04Pipeline

Method

01 Scraping — retrieval of 12,522 broadcasts, 01/1991 – 07/2026
02 Whisper ASR — local transcription of ~3,100 hours of audio
03 Transcription diagnostics — automated quality checks, against official broadcast subtitles where available
04 Salience measure — monthly, dictionary-based; era-specific vocabularies pre-2005; validated against IFEM InfoMonitor
05 Trivariate VAR with Granger-causality tests — ~400 monthly observations, alongside BAMF asylum statistics and the Politbarometer "most important problems" series. Granger tests establish temporal precedence, not full causal identification
