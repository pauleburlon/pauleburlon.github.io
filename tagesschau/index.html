Tagesschau Broadcast Corpus and Migration Salience

Ongoing, Independent research, Since 2025, Sole author

Public concern about immigration in Germany has surged and receded in ways that asylum numbers alone cannot explain. Concern peaked with record asylum applications in 1992/93 and 2015/16, but barely moved when a million Ukrainians arrived in 2022. Does television news mirror real-world change, drive concern beyond it, or follow its audience? Reality, coverage, and public opinion have to be modelled as a system.

The empirical basis is a full-text corpus of the spoken Tagesschau um 20 Uhr (ARD), Germany’s most-watched television news programme, arguably the closest approximation to a shared national information environment. No comparable public corpus of the broadcast exists.

The corpus

12,393 broadcasts, 3,184 hours of audio, 25.9 million words, 1991–2026, archive gap Sep 2006 – Sep 2007, transcribed locally with Whisper

Scraped from the archive: the evening “tagesschau um 20 Uhr”, plus the daily rerun “tagesschau vor 20 Jahren”, which recovers 1991–2006.

† The corpus itself cannot be published (ARD broadcast rights); the processing pipeline can.

First look

Coverage and public concern move together: r = 0.82 in levels and 0.44 in month-to-month changes (2000–2026; concern = share naming immigration a top problem in the Politbarometer).

  • Coverage does not simply mirror arrivals. In June 2018 (the government crisis over border rejections) it jumps to 7.5 while asylum applications stay flat. In 2022, about 1.1 million people arrive from Ukraine, yet coverage stays at 0.8, against 4.6 in 2015–16.
  • In June 2018 and 2022, concern followed coverage, not arrivals.
  • The vocabulary shifts: “Ausländer” falls from 21 % to 4 % of all hits between the 1990s and today, while “Migration” rises from 6 % to 20 %.

Limits: archive gap Sep 2006 – Sep 2007; a dictionary counts words, not tone; in 2015–16 many asylum applications were filed months late.

Planned validation

Next, the coverage measure will be checked against the hand-coded airtime shares of the IFEM InfoMonitor, which reach back only to 2005.

Three migration episodes

The puzzle: public concern tracked the first two episodes but barely moved for the third.

  • 1992/93

    Asylum debate. Record asylum applications; concern peaked.

  • 2015/16

    Refugee inflow. Record inflow; concern peaked again.

  • 2022

    Ukraine displacement. A million arrivals; concern barely moved.

Method

  1. Scraping
    12,393 broadcasts, 1991–2026, including the daily rerun “tagesschau vor 20 Jahren” for 1991–2006
  2. Whisper ASR
    Local transcription of 3,184 hours of audio, 25.9 million words
  3. Coverage measure
    Monthly, dictionary-based: migration words per 1,000 spoken words
  4. Linkage
    Politbarometer problem awareness (from 2000) and asylum applications (BAMF, then Eurostat)

Still open. Is coverage the cause, or do events reach the news before the survey? Next: match coverage to survey dates to test temporal order, validate transcripts against official broadcast subtitles, and validate the measure against IFEM InfoMonitor airtime.
